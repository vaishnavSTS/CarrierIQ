How CarrierIQ works

From public FMCSA records to a reviewable carrier profile

CarrierIQ pulls a motor carrier’s federal records, keeps every version it sees, and turns changes and patterns into review signals. Each signal shows the exact records behind it. This page shows how the pieces fit together.

Intelligence engine

Six fixed rules. Each signal must carry evidence, or it is not saved.

Authority changeRevoked, suspended, reinstated, pending revocation
Insurance changeNew insurer, coverage change, cancellation, gaps
Identity changeName, address, phone, email, officers
Fleet consistencyRegistered trucks vs. trucks seen on inspections
Safety trendOut-of-service rate over the last 12 months vs. the year before
Shared VINSame vehicle inspected under another USDOT number
served by the FastAPI backend at /api/v1

Background worker

A separate Python program that keeps data fresh without anyone opening a carrier.

  1. Every 15 minutes, finds carriers with data older than 24 hours.
  2. Queues a refresh job for each (at most 50 per round).
  3. Runs each job through steps 1–4, the same as opening the carrier.
  4. If a source is down, retries after 1, 2 and 4 minutes, then marks the job failed.

The job list lives in the same database; no extra queue service is needed. “Refresh now” on a carrier page adds a job straight away.

Walkthrough

What happens when someone looks up a carrier

  1. SearchType a USDOT number, an MC/MX/FF docket or a company name. Carriers not yet in CarrierIQ are looked up live in the FMCSA census.
  2. Fetch if neededIf the carrier is new, or its data is more than 24 hours old, CarrierIQ fetches its census record, inspections, authority and insurance from FMCSA. If FMCSA is down, it shows the stored data and marks it as stale.
  3. Keep the originalEvery row received is saved untouched before it is cleaned up, and any value that changed is added to the history.
  4. Decode vehiclesNew VINs from the inspections are sent to NHTSA once, and their year, make and model are kept.
  5. Rebuild timeline and signalsAuthority and insurance events go on the timeline, and the six rules re-run. A signal reviewed earlier keeps its review.
  6. Show the profileThe header shows authority, insurance and how many signals are open. Each tab loads its own part.

Ground rules

Principles built into the system

Evidence first

Every signal lists the source records behind it, with dates and record numbers.

Nothing is erased

Raw records and earlier values are kept, so “what did this carrier look like on a date?” can be answered.

Careful language

Signals say “requires review” or “potential relationship”, never “fraud”. They prompt a review; they are not findings.

Fresh but resilient

Data older than 24 hours is re-fetched. When a source is down, stored data is shown and marked stale.

Technology: React, TypeScript and Tailwind in the browser; Python, FastAPI and SQLAlchemy on the server; PostgreSQL on Supabase for storage; Docker for running it all together.
